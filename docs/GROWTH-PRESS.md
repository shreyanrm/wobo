# Press, awards and being the named answer

**The owner, 2026-09-09:** *"If we could somehow get ourselves published on all the top media
websites, Forbes, TechCrunch, Awwwards, Wall Street Journal or whatever it may be, all those can get
us on top as well."*

Right, and for a reason beyond vanity. The search research found that "Wobo" as an entity is already
taken in every answer engine by **wobo.ai** (a job-search app with an App Store listing, Trustpilot
reviews and a Product Hunt page) and **wobo.app** (digital workbooks). Ask any engine today about
"Wobo AI tutor" and it answers about the job-search product, and one engine says flatly that Wobo AI
is not an AI tutor. Press, awards and listings are how that changes: an answer engine names what
enough independent sources corroborate. So this is not a vanity chase. It is entity work with a
by-product of traffic.

## 1. What each outlet actually needs

| Outlet | What gets you in | Realistic for us | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Awwwards, CSS Design Awards, FWA** | you submit your own site and pay a fee; the judging is on design and craft | **yes, and soon.** The site is design-led and the drawn ink is unlike anything in the category. Site of the Day is a real target once the public pages are pre-rendered and fast | after the ink lands and the site is static |
| **Product Hunt** | a launch day you run yourself; needs a good video and a live product | **yes.** One clear video of Wobo drawing an answer on a real page beats any copy | launch week |
| **Indian tech press** (YourStory, Inc42, Entrackr, Analytics India Magazine, Economic Times Tech, Moneycontrol, The Ken) | a founder story, a funding round, real numbers, or a genuinely new mechanic | **yes.** This is the reachable tier and it is the tier our parents read | at launch, then at every real number |
| **TechCrunch** | funding, notable traction, or a mechanic a reporter has not seen | **only with a hook.** "A tutor that draws on the page like a teacher" is a hook; "another AI tutor" is not | at a round or at a number |
| **Forbes** | contributor pieces (pay to play, low value) or a staff piece needing the same hooks; Forbes India and 30 Under 30 are separate paths | later, and never buy a contributor slot | after traction |
| **Wall Street Journal, The Economist** | a trend they are already writing, with you as the illustrative case | **not a target.** Be a source, not a subject | year two |

**The rule that governs all of it:** a journalist writes about a number, a person, or a first. We have
one first worth telling (Wobo draws the explanation live on the learner's own page and on a photo of
their homework), and we will have numbers. Nothing is pitched before the ink is good, because a
demonstration that misfires is worse than silence.

## 2. What we do, in order

1. **Own the entity everywhere first.** Wikidata item, LinkedIn company page, Crunchbase, Product Hunt,
   G2 and Capterra listings, Play Store and App Store when the shells ship, a Google Business Profile
   for Dot eVentures, and a consistent one-sentence description on every one of them, identical to the
   site's. Same name, same logo, same line. This is what makes an engine say "Wobo is an AI tutor for
   Indian school students" instead of the job app.
2. **Build the press kit** on heywobo.com/press, from docs/copy/press-kit.md (every string written, to be used verbatim and identically everywhere): the one-liner, the hundred-word and three-hundred-word
   descriptions, founder name and photo, the logo in SVG and PNG, three screenshots, a sixty-second
   film of a real lesson being drawn, the numbers we can prove, and a contact address. Journalists take
   what is easy. Every AI engine reads it too.
3. **Submit to the design awards** the week the site is static and fast. Awwwards Site of the Day,
   CSS Design Awards, FWA, and the smaller galleries (Godly, Land-book, Httpster). Each is a link from
   a high-authority domain and a badge a parent finds reassuring.
4. **Run the Product Hunt launch** as a real event: a two-minute film, the founder answering every
   comment for twenty-four hours, and the launch page pointing at a chapter page rather than the home
   page.
5. **Be a source before you are a subject.** Answer journalist requests (Qwoted, Featured, SourceBottle
   and the Indian equivalents) on AI in education, children's data, and what an AI tutor can and cannot
   do. Three quotes in three months makes the fourth pitch land.
6. **Pitch the Indian tech press at launch** with the film and one number, individually and by name,
   never a blast. Then again at each real milestone: the first ten thousand lessons drawn, a board
   fully covered, a school using it.
7. **Publish our own research.** The syllabus observer sees which chapters learners get stuck on across
   boards. Once there is enough of it and it is anonymous, that is a story no one else in India can
   write, and it is the kind of thing that gets cited rather than covered.

## 3. What we never do

Buy a Forbes contributor slot or a paid "as featured in" badge. Pitch before the product survives a
demonstration. Send the same email to fifty journalists. Claim a number we cannot show. Put a child's
face or work in a press asset. Every one of those costs more in trust than it buys in traffic.

## 4. What it is worth, honestly

Press is a spike; the chapter pages and the answer engines are the compounding asset. One TechCrunch
piece is a week of traffic and a permanent citation; a thousand good chapter pages are a year of
traffic and the reason an engine names us. So press runs beside the search work in
docs/GROWTH-SEARCH.md, not instead of it. The one place press is decisive is the entity problem above:
until enough sources say what Wobo is, no amount of on-site work makes an answer engine say it for us.
