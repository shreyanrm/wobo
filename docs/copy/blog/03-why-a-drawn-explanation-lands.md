**Watching a diagram appear stroke by stroke tells you why each line is there, and that is the part a finished diagram has already thrown away.**

Think about what a good teacher does when you say you do not get it. They do not open a book at the finished figure. They pick up a pen, put down one line, and say what that line is. Then the next one, and why it goes there and not somewhere else. By the time the figure is complete you have not been shown a figure; you have been walked through a decision, several times over.

The finished figure is the residue of that walk. It is useful once you already understand it, which is exactly when you no longer need it.

## The order is the argument

Take a circle theorem, or a free body diagram, or the moment in a long division where the remainder comes down. In every one of them the thing that is hard is not the final picture. It is knowing which mark to make next and why.

A drawing made in front of you carries that order in the only way it can be carried: in time. The angle is marked, then the radius, then the second radius, and the reason the second radius matters is visible because it arrives after the first and changes what you are looking at. Nothing about the completed figure tells you which of its lines was the idea and which were bookkeeping.

This is also why copying a worked example out of a book so often produces nothing. You reproduce the marks in the order they are printed, which is not the order anybody thought them.

A recorded explanation keeps the order, which is why a good video is genuinely good. What it cannot keep is you. It was finished before you arrived, so it cannot slow down at the line you are stuck on, and pausing it is not the same act as asking a question. You can rewind a video to the third minute. You cannot ask it why it did that.

## The pen can point at your line, not at a line

The second thing a drawing does, and the reason this is not a style choice, is that it can happen on your page.

A general explanation has to be about a general case. A pen circling the third line of your own working can be about the exact place where your sign flipped. Those are different acts. One of them is teaching material and the other is teaching you, and the difference is not effort or warmth, it is what the explanation is allowed to refer to.

## Where drawing is the wrong form, and what happens then

Drawing is one of several things a tutor should be able to do, and treating it as the whole job would be its own mistake.

Some things want a short film, because they move: a wave passing through a medium, an eclipse, a mechanism turning. Some want a model you can drag, because the understanding is in the rotation. Some want to be said out loud slowly, because the difficulty is in the wording of the question rather than in the mathematics. Some want practice and nothing else, because the idea is understood and the hand is not yet quick. And after any of them, something has to remember what did not stick and bring it back later, or the evening was entertainment.

So the honest claim is narrow and it is about form, not about medium. For the class of ideas where the reasoning has an order, watching the order happen is a better explanation than being shown the result of it. For other classes of idea, something else wins, and the tutor's job is to pick.

## What we do not claim

We do not claim that a drawn explanation raises marks. We have no study, and a number without a study behind it is a number somebody made up. What we can say is what the product does, which is that the explanation is generated for the question in front of it, that the working is drawn as it is reasoned rather than replayed from a library, and that when one explanation does not land the next one changes its method rather than repeating itself louder.

A claim that any product improves results by a percentage is a factual claim, and a factual claim is worth a study, a sample size and the name of whoever paid for it. That is fair to ask of us, and our honest answer today is that we do not have one.

## The cheapest test of any of this

Take something your child said they did not understand this week. Find whatever explanation they were given: a video, a page, a solution. Watch it and ask one question afterwards, which is not "do you get it now" but "why is that line there".

If the explanation carried the order, they can answer. If it only carried the result, they will describe the picture back to you. That is the whole distinction, and it takes about two minutes to run.
